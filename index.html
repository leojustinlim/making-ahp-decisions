What is AHP?

AHP (Analytic Hierarchy Process) is a structured way to make decisions when several factors matter. It breaks a complicated choice into smaller comparisons, helps you decide how important each factor is, and brings those judgements together to rank your options.

Key terms, made simple

Alternatives
The different options you are choosing between.
Criteria
The factors that matter when making your decision. Subcriteria are smaller factors grouped under a broader criterion.
Pairwise comparison
Looking at just two criteria or options at a time and deciding which you prefer, and by how much. Traditional AHP uses a 1–9 importance scale.
Weights
Numbers that show how important each criterion is relative to the others. The criterion weights add up to 100%.
Consistency ratio (CR)
A check of how logically consistent your pairwise judgements are. A lower ratio generally means fewer contradictions; 0.10 or less is a common guideline, not a guarantee of a good decision.
Ranking
The order of your options based on the weights and evaluations you entered. The highest-ranked option is the strongest fit for your stated priorities, not necessarily the best in every situation.

How it works: Define your options and criteria → assign importance using pairwise comparisons (or direct percentages) → evaluate each option → review rankings and trade-offs.

Traditional AHP calculates weights from pairwise comparisons. Manual weighting is a faster alternative if you already know the percentages you want to use; it does not have a pairwise consistency ratio.
